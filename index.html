Featured project

Kaputle Workbench

A two-cell flashlight you can take apart, rebuild step by step and switch on. Every part is modelled to scale and comes off in the order it would in real life: the tail cap before the batteries, the head before the bulb.

It only lights up when the circuit is actually complete. Unscrew the tail cap while it's on and it goes dark. Take the head off and the bare bulb glows with no beam.

What it does

  • Unscrews, slides and lifts parts out with realistic rules for what has to come off first
  • Close-up inspection of any visible part, with what it does and what to look for
  • A seven-step guided build with camera framing for each step
  • Real circuit logic, a light beam, and workshop sounds

How it's built

  • Three.js
  • WebGL
  • JavaScript
  • Web Audio API
  • Procedural geometry
  • Procedural textures
  • Static hosting

Round parts are 2D profiles spun on a virtual lathe. Springs, threads and the filament are tubes along helix curves. Metal, rubber and glass use physically based materials.

My role

I came up with the concept and directed the product: how it should feel, how parts come apart, the guided build, and every round of polish and testing. I built it with Claude Code as my coding partner.
